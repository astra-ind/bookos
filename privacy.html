Privacy

Last updated: September 2026

The short version

Your books never leave your machine unless you send them somewhere. BookOS is a native desktop application with no accounts and no telemetry. What follows is the precise version.

Where manuscripts are stored

Every book lives in a .bookos folder you choose, on your disk: the full project as human-readable Markdown plus a local SQLite working database. The application reads and writes those folders and nothing else beyond its own settings and caches. Backups are archives of the same project data, stored locally, and never contain credentials.

What is sent to AI providers

BookOS does not include AI by default. If you configure a provider — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, a compatible endpoint, or a local model — then the AI features you invoke send a task-specific context packet (for example: your constitution, relevant canon, the chapter being worked on) to that provider, exactly as when you use that provider anywhere else. The destination is your account at your provider, under their terms and privacy policy. No provider is configured, no data leaves. A built-in offline embedder powers semantic search locally.

How API keys are handled

Keys are stored in your operating system's credential vault (Windows DPAPI, macOS Keychain, or libsecret on Linux). They are never written to project files, never stored in the application database, never included in backups, and never transmitted anywhere except to the provider you attached them to.

Account data

There are no accounts. The application keeps a local library registry so the home screen can list your books; it contains paths and timestamps, stays on your machine, and can be cleared with the app.

Backups and uninstall

Backups contain your project, never your keys. Uninstalling the application does not delete your book folders — your writing is independent of the software by design.
